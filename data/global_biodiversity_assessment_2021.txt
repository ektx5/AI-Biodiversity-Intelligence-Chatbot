Global Biodiversity Assessment (2021)

Focus: Land Use and Habitat Fragmentation

Findings:
1. Habitat Fragmentation: Large-scale monoculture farming creates biological deserts, severing migratory routes and isolating animal populations. Land use changes account for over 50% of recent biodiversity declines.
2. Corridors and Mixed-Crop Systems: Transitioning from intensive monoculture to mixed-crop systems creates natural corridors for wildlife. This reduces habitat fragmentation, promoting natural pest control by beneficial insects. Insect diversity has been shown to increase by up to 50% in mixed-crop environments compared to adjacent monoculture plots.
3. Pollution and Soil Health: Runoff from synthetic fertilizers in intensive farming degrades soil health (lowering pH and organic matter) and pollutes nearby waterways, leading to eutrophication which drastically reduces aquatic species richness.
