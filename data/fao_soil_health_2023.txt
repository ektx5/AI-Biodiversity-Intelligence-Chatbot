FAO Report on Sustainable Agriculture and Soil Health (2023)

Abstract:
This report highlights the critical link between soil health and biodiversity in agricultural landscapes.

Key Findings:
1. Cover Crops and Soil Organic Carbon (SOC): The introduction of legume-based cover crops (such as clover and vetch) increases soil organic carbon by approximately 15-25% over a period of 2-3 years. This increase in SOC significantly improves microbial diversity in the soil and provides better support for local pollinator populations.
2. Monoculture Risks: Monoculture systems, particularly wheat or corn grown continuously, deplete soil nutrients and lead to a 40% reduction in soil microbiome diversity within a decade.
3. Agroforestry: Integrating trees with crops (agroforestry) or intercropping improves soil moisture retention by 30% in semi-arid regions, providing a buffer against low rainfall. This practice directly correlates with higher species richness of both flora and fauna.

Recommendations:
- Transition from monoculture to mixed-crop or agroforestry systems to enhance carbon sequestration and biological activity.
